## Two restrictions worth remembering

**Pexels and Unsplash both bar resale and compilation.** The Unsplash licence
says outright that it does not grant the right to compile their images to
replicate a competing service, and Pexels bars selling unaltered copies. A city
wallpaper inside a marketplace app is comfortably inside that. An app whose
product *is* a wallpaper library would not be.

**Freedom of panorama in the US covers buildings, not sculptures** (17 U.S.C.
§120(a)). A photograph of a building visible from a public place is fine
regardless of who owns the building; a photograph of a copyrighted sculpture is
a derivative work no matter what licence the photograph itself carries. One
otherwise-good Redwood City candidate was dropped for exactly this — its subject
was a Beniamino Bufano sculpture.

## The two the operator picked himself, 2026-08-11

He sent a DuckDuckGo image link for each — the Nut Tree for Vacaville and the
C&H sugar refinery for Crockett. Both pointed at copyrighted stock (Expedia and
KQED/Getty), so neither file could be used; the subjects here are the same ones
from Commons instead.

Crockett is the Carquinez bridges rather than the sugar refinery. The only
usable refinery photograph on Commons is 1024x768 — a third of the pixels this
frame needs, and upscaling it would look worse than no photograph at all. The
bridges are the other thing Crockett is, they sit in the same frame as the
refinery from most angles, and the tower makes a far better portrait crop.

Vacaville is 465KB against the 230KB the encoder aims for. It hit the quality
floor and still came out large, because the picture is midday foliage and
gravel — thousands of small high-contrast edges, which is the worst case for
any codec. It is not a quality setting that can be turned down without banding
the sky; it is what that photograph costs. Only Vacaville's own page pays it.

## They were too small, so they are super-resolved

Every link he sends is a small landscape thumbnail — 454x604 for Orinda,
1200x630 for Lafayette, 800x600 for Vacaville. This frame is 1320x2868
PORTRAIT, so the usable part of a wide thumbnail is a narrow vertical slice: a
1200x630 image yields 289 pixels across, needing a 4.5x blow-up. That is what
made them unusable and why they were being replaced.

`tools/upscale.py` runs EDSR x4 over anything that would need more than a 1.15x
stretch, before the crop. It is a super-resolution network rather than a
sharpen or a better resize — side by side at 4x on the Orinda frame it holds
the neon tubing as edges where Lanczos turns it into a glow. The encoder calls
it automatically; nothing to remember.

It is slow. Six of these took ten minutes, most of it Orinda and Vacaville,
which start smallest and therefore have the most to invent. That is a fine
trade for something run once per city.

## Where the gaps are

Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Saratoga, Redwood City, Pleasant Hill and El Cerrito are
daytime shots, against a set that is otherwise night. Pleasant Hill and El
Cerrito are daytime because NO night or dusk photograph of either city exists
under an acceptable licence anywhere searched — Commons by category, geosearch
and EXIF sweep, plus Unsplash, Pexels and Pixabay. Two specific subjects have
nothing at all and were dropped rather than substituted: the Cerrito Theater
marquee and Napa's Uptown Theatre marquee. No freely licensed night photograph of a landmark
in those four exists on Wikimedia Commons, Unsplash, Pexels or Pixabay — that
was searched by category, by full text and by coordinates, and the gap is real
rather than a search failure. Grading one of them to look like night would still
be an adaptation, so the ShareAlike obligation would follow it. Shooting them is
the only clean upgrade.

The Arizona set added 2026-08-18 is daytime for the same reason and one more:
the four he linked himself are the subjects he chose, and the rule is to use
the link he sends. Glendale is the weakest of the ten — a road with the stadium
behind it rather than the stadium — and it is there because no freely licensed
photograph of the building itself, at a size a portrait crop survives, turned up
under a licence that permits commercial use. It is honestly Glendale, which is
the bar, but it is the one to replace first if he sends a link.
